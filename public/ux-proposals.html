UX Proposals

Navigation: 6 items → 4 items

A volunteer board president has 10 minutes at night. Every tap counts. The current 6-item nav with mobile overflow means the two most important things — Cases and financial data — compete for attention with governance and archives. The proposal: 4 items that map to what board members actually do.

Why this matters: Cases is OneTwo's core differentiator — no other HOA tool has jurisdictional step-by-step resolution workflows. But Cases is currently buried inside Board Room. Promoting it to primary nav makes the product's value immediately visible.

Today View: Action-items first

The current Dashboard shows 4 KPI cards, attention pills, fiduciary alerts, delinquency aging, budget alerts, meetings, and activity — all at once. For a board president with 10 minutes, the question is: "What do I need to do right now?" Everything else is noise. The proposed Today view answers that question in the first scroll.

Design principle: Only show an alert banner if something is critically wrong (insurance expired, fiduciary duty at risk). Action items are tappable — each one takes you to the exact screen to resolve it. The 3-stat strip gives a pulse check without taking up card space. Financial detail (aging, budget variance, charts) moves to Money.

Case Creation: 10 categories → 4 groups

The current case creation wizard shows 10 category tiles + a custom option, then situation picker, then approach selection (pre-legal vs legal vs self-represented). For a board member creating their first case, it's overwhelming. The proposal groups categories into 4 intuitive buckets, defaults to step-by-step resolution (the pre-legal path), and shows a contextual "next step" banner on each case instead of the full step list.

Key insight: The approach selection (pre-legal vs legal vs self-represented) is a power feature that confuses new users. 90%+ of cases should start as pre-legal. The proposal removes the choice — every case starts as step-by-step resolution. A banner says "If this escalates, you can add Legal Counsel path anytime." Power users still get the full workflow; new users don't need to understand the distinction.

Building: 4 pages merged into tabs

The Building, Board Room, Association Team, and The Archives are currently 4 separate top-level pages. The proposal merges them into a single "Building" page with tabs: Overview, People, Governance, Documents, Units, and Comms. This reduces cognitive load — everything about your building is in one place.

Rationale: Board members don't think "I need the Board Room" — they think "I need to check on my building's meeting schedule." The Building tab becomes the single source of truth for everything about the property: who runs it, how it's governed, what documents exist, what units are there, and how to communicate with owners.

Terminology Simplification

OneTwo uses professional HOA terminology throughout the app. While this signals expertise to industry insiders, it creates friction for volunteer board members who manage their building's compliance alongside a day job. The proposal: use plain language in the UI, keep the legal precision in the step content and legal citations where it matters.

Principle: The navigation should use language a first-time board president understands. The content inside cases and compliance items keeps full legal terminology and DC Code citations — that's where precision matters and where OneTwo's expertise shows.
