Feature spotlight

Other software collects data and stops. A manager charges and answers to no one. ONE two does the job, proves it, and costs less.

A ledger tells you what happened. A runbook makes the next thing happen: the fee is charged, the letter goes out, the filing is prepared, the receipt is filed. Four places you can see the difference.

01 · Escalation

Every need has a path, from a friendly notice to the courthouse.

A late payment, a rules violation, a leak in a unit, an insurance claim. Each one starts with the friendly steps: a notice, a reminder, a fee where the bylaws allow it, a cure period. Most end there, and most of that runs itself. If one doesn't, it moves up on the timeline the bylaws and DC Code set, and every letter, vote and receipt from the first notice comes with it. The board can take it as far as Small Claims on its own. Counsel comes in only when the board says so.

  • Pre-legal. Notices, reminders, fees and cure periods, sent on time and by the right channel. Most end here, and most of it runs itself.
  • Self-directed legal. The certified demand, the hearing notice, the lien and the Small Claims packet, drafted from the file and voted by the board.
  • Legal escalation. When the board decides, one packet to the association's attorney with every letter, receipt, vote and ledger line inside.
02 · Dues and fees

Dues collected in the app, into the association's own account.

Assessments post on the 1st. Owners pay by bank or card from their phone, or autopay takes it. The late fee applies after the grace period your bylaws set. Every payment lands in the ledger and on the owner's statement, and 30 days past due hands off to collections. The money goes straight to the association's bank through Stripe. No manager in the middle.

  • Billing runs itself. Assessments, late fees, special assessments and the reserve transfer, on their dates.
  • Owners see one number. Balance, next due date, statements, and a Pay button. A payment plan counts as on time.
  • The books close. Payments reconcile themselves; the month closes on the 1st with a statement every owner can read.
03 · Notices and letters

Email, the portal, or certified mail. The app sends what the law needs.

Every notice starts from a template the app fills in with the names, amounts and dates. It posts to the portal and emails every owner with an address on file. The owners who want paper get first class. A demand letter goes certified with return receipt, and the USPS tracking and the PDF are filed in the Archive. Postage at cost.

  • Right channel, every time. Meeting notices inside the legal window, budget notices 30 days ahead, demands by certified mail.
  • Nothing to print. The mail vendor prints, stuffs and posts it; first class and certified mail all at a direct cost to you. No upcharge from us.
  • Proof, filed. Tracking, delivery and the letter itself land in the Archive against the obligation they satisfy.
04 · Runbooks

The work starts itself. You are asked only to sign, pay or decide.

Thirty-two runbooks watch the calendar and the building. The month closes on the 1st. The insurance renewal starts 60 days before the policy expires, filings 45 days before they are due, the budget 90 days before the fiscal year, collections at 30 days past due, the snow plan at the first forecast under freezing. Each one gathers what it needs, does the steps it can, and brings in the right board member only where a person has to sign, pay or decide. The building stays in good standing without anyone keeping a list.

  • Started by the date or the event. A policy expiry, a vendor's first payment, a request from an owner, a move, a forecast. Nothing waits for someone to remember.
  • Done where it can be. Letters filled, fees charged, W-9s collected, 1099s e-filed, inspectors booked, statements sent. The step that needs a person is a Home card for that seat.
  • Closed with proof. The receipt goes in the Archive, Compliance moves, and the owners see a building in good standing.
